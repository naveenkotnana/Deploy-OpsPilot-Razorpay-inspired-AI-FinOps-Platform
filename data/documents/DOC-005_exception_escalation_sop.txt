1. SEVERITY LADDER
Severity is INFO, LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, or CRITICAL. Severity is assigned by
deterministic rule, never by a language model and never by an unsupervised
model score alone.

2. REVENUE MOVEMENT THRESHOLDS
A month-over-month revenue movement of 8 percent or more is LOW, 15 percent or
more is MEDIUM, 25 percent or more is HIGH, and 40 percent or more is CRITICAL.
Thresholds apply to the absolute value of the movement, so a sharp increase is
escalated the same as a sharp decrease.

3. EXCEPTION COUNT THRESHOLDS
One or more validation exceptions in a location month is LOW, five or more is
MEDIUM, ten or more is HIGH, and twenty or more is CRITICAL.

4. MACHINE LEARNING DETECTIONS
An IsolationForest detection with no corresponding rule breach is capped at LOW
severity. An unsupervised score is a prompt to investigate, not grounds to
escalate.

5. ALERT CREATION
An anomaly at MEDIUM severity or above creates an alert. Alerts below MEDIUM are
recorded as anomalies only and are reviewed in the monthly data quality cycle.

6. INVESTIGATION
Investigation gathers SQL evidence, analytics evidence, and policy evidence
before any conclusion is drawn. Where evidence does not support a root cause,
the investigation returns INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE. A root cause is never asserted
from correlation alone.

7. HUMAN APPROVAL
Incident creation, ticket creation, and notification dispatch are sensitive
actions and require approval by a Manager or Admin role. An Analyst may
investigate but may not approve. A rejected approval terminates the workflow and
no action executes.

8. ESCALATION PATH
MEDIUM escalates to the Operations Analyst queue. HIGH escalates to the
Operations Manager within one business day. CRITICAL escalates to the Operations
Manager immediately and requires a same-day incident record.

9. IDEMPOTENCY
An action is keyed by workflow and action type. A repeated request for an action
already executed is recorded as DUPLICATE_SKIPPED and does not create a second
incident, ticket, or notification.
